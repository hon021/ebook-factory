### **Capítulo 1: Antes de Empezar - ¿Qué Puede Hacer la IA?** (5.2K palabras)

**Objetivo de aprendizaje:** Entender capacidades y limitaciones reales

**Estructura:**
1. What AI can do TODAY (not tomorrow)
   - Write and edit text
   - Summarize and extract key points
   - Organize and structure information
   - Brainstorm and ideate
   - Explain complex topics simply

2. What AI CANNOT do
   - Access real-time information (no live data)
   - Access your files (unless you paste content)
   - Make decisions for you
   - Create legally binding documents without review
   - Understand specific internal company knowledge

3. When AI is likely WRONG
   - Factual details (dates, names, numbers)
   - Industry-specific terminology
   - New developments (after training cutoff)
   - Legal or financial advice

4. Real example: Email draft
   - Good for: Grammar, clarity, tone
   - Risky for: Specific numbers, sensitive info, legal language

5. Key insight: "AI is a tool, not a replacement"

**Ejercicio:** "Describe one task you do weekly. Which parts could AI help with? Which parts need YOU?"

**Checklist:** 
- [ ] I can name 3 things AI does well
- [ ] I can name 3 things AI does poorly
- [ ] I understand why I need to review AI's work

### **Capítulo 2: Tu Primera Tarea - Cómo Dar una Buena Instrucción** (5.5K palabras)

**Objetivo de aprendizaje:** Craft clear prompts that get good results

**Estructura:**
1. The formula that works
   - CONTEXT: "I'm a marketing manager"
   - TASK: "Write an email to a client"
   - DETAILS: "About their Q4 budget request"
   - TONE: "Professional but friendly"
   - LENGTH: "Maximum 150 words"

2. Why context matters (+ example showing difference)
   - With context: Good email
   - Without context: Generic email

3. The 3-sentence rule
   - Bad: 2-word prompt
   - Better: 1 clear sentence per aspect
   - Best: Short, specific, actionable

4. Real example: Summarize a meeting
   - Bad prompt & result
   - Good prompt & result
   - Why the difference

5. Common mistakes
   - Too vague ("make it better")
   - Too much info (paste 5-page document)
   - Wrong language ("write in English" if AI is trained on both)
   - Contradictions ("short but detailed")

6. The experiment: Same task, 3 ways
   - Try it yourself now

**Ejercicio:** "Write 3 different prompts for the same task. See which result is best. Why?"

**Checklist:**
- [ ] I can write a prompt with context + task + tone
- [ ] I understand why detail helps
- [ ] I know the difference between good and bad prompts

### **Capítulo 3: Caso 1 - Redactar un Correo Profesional** (5.8K palabras)

**Objetivo de aprendizaje:** Use AI to write email drafts you can actually send

**Estructura:**
1. The email problem (Why this matters)
   - Email takes 10+ minutes to write
   - Tone is hard to get right
   - Overthinking leads to procrastination

2. The AI solution (Step-by-step)
   - Step 1: Describe who you're writing to
   - Step 2: Explain what you want them to do
   - Step 3: Mention any constraints (length, formality)
   - Step 4: Paste into AI
   - Step 5: Read the draft
   - Step 6: Personalize (add details, remove generic phrases)

3. Complete worked example
   - Task: "Request deadline extension from boss"
   - Prompt (exact prompt you can copy)
   - AI result (what you get)
   - What to fix (personalization + details)
   - Final version (ready to send)

4. Common email types
   - Asking for something
   - Saying no politely
   - Explaining a problem
   - Thanking someone
   - Following up

5. The checklist (Before hitting send)
   - [ ] Did I add my name/signature?
   - [ ] Is the tone right for this person?
   - [ ] Did I remove generic phrases?
   - [ ] Does it have specific details (dates, numbers)?
   - [ ] Did I spell-check the whole thing?

6. When AI email FAILS
   - Example: Sensitive news (needs your voice)
   - Example: Legal content (needs lawyer review)
   - Example: Very informal contact (needs personal touch)

**Ejercicio:** "Write an email prompt. Get AI result. Fix it. Send it. Report: Did it work?"

**Checklist:**
- [ ] I can write a clear email prompt
- [ ] I know how to personalize an AI draft
- [ ] I understand which emails need extra review

### **Capítulo 4: Caso 2 - Resumir un Documento Largo** (5.5K palabras)

**Objetivo de aprendizaje:** Extract key points without reading 10 pages

**Estructura:**
1. The summary problem
   - Your manager sends you 15-page report
   - You need the key points in 5 minutes
   - You're too busy to read carefully
   - AI can help, but results vary

2. The AI solution
   - Step 1: Copy the document
   - Step 2: Ask for specific format (bullet points, one paragraph, categories)
   - Step 3: Specify length (3 bullets, 1 page, 200 words)
   - Step 4: Paste into AI
   - Step 5: Review against original
   - Step 6: Verify the numbers/facts

3. Complete worked example
   - Task: "Summarize Q3 sales report for boss briefing"
   - Original document (1 page excerpt)
   - Prompt (exact)
   - AI result
   - What to verify (check numbers, dates)
   - Final summary (ready for boss)

4. Summary formats that work
   - Bullet points (easiest)
   - One-paragraph summary
   - Executive summary (key insights + recommendations)
   - Timeline format
   - By-department format

5. When summaries FAIL
   - Example: Missing critical point
   - Example: Wrong number quoted
   - Example: Nuance lost

6. The verification step
   - Read original again: 5 minutes
   - Spot check key facts: 2 minutes
   - Total: 7 minutes (vs 20 reading normally)

7. Real productivity gain
   - Before: 20 min reading + understanding
   - After: 2 min AI + 5 min verification = 7 min total
   - Gain: 13 minutes per document

**Ejercicio:** "Find a 5+ page document. Summarize with AI. Verify. Did it capture the essentials?"

**Checklist:**
- [ ] I can paste content safely (no sensitive data)
- [ ] I know which format works for my purpose
- [ ] I can spot-check facts quickly
- [ ] I save 15+ minutes per document

### **Capítulo 5: Caso 3 - Organizar una Reunión** (5.6K palabras)

**Objetivo de aprendizaje:** Plan structured meetings with AI help

**Estructura:**
1. The meeting problem
   - You have 5 people, 1 hour, no clear agenda
   - Topics overlap and meander
   - Time gets wasted
   - No clear outcomes or next steps

2. The AI solution
   - Step 1: List what needs to be discussed
   - Step 2: Estimate time per topic
   - Step 3: Ask AI to organize the agenda
   - Step 4: Refine (add time buffers, specify decisions needed)
   - Step 5: Send to attendees 24 hours early

3. Complete worked example
   - Task: "Plan 1-hour team sync with 5 people"
   - Topics (messy list)
   - Prompt (exact)
   - AI agenda result
   - Your refinement (added time, added objectives)
   - Final agenda (sent to team)

4. Structured meeting template
   ```
   1. Opening (5 min): Objective & outcomes
   2. Topic A (15 min): Decision/discussion/update
   3. Topic B (15 min): Same
   4. Topic C (15 min): Same
   5. Closing (5 min): Recap + next steps
   ```

5. Decision vs discussion vs update
   - DECISION meetings: Need clear question + options
   - DISCUSSION meetings: Need pre-reading + facilitator
   - UPDATE meetings: Can be async (don't do in meeting)

6. AI helps you pre-think
   - By organizing, you catch missing info
   - By setting timers, you stay on track
   - By writing outcomes, you're clear on purpose

7. Remote vs in-person
   - Remote: Share agenda in chat before meeting
   - In-person: Print for everyone

**Ejercicio:** "Plan your next meeting with AI. Did people finish on time? Clear outcomes?"

**Checklist:**
- [ ] I have a clear agenda with time allocations
- [ ] I've assigned who leads each topic
- [ ] I've defined what "success" means (decision vs just talk)
- [ ] I sent agenda 24 hours in advance
- [ ] After meeting: Did we stay on time? Hit objectives?

   - You take notes during meetings/calls

   - You can't remember what to DO
   - Tasks get forgotten

2. The AI solution
   - Step 1: Paste your messy notes
   - Step 2: Ask for "Action Items" format
   - Step 3: Ask to assign owners and deadlines
   - Step 4: Review for missing context
   - Step 5: Share with team or calendar

3. Complete worked example
   - Task: "Extract action items from meeting notes"
   - Original messy notes (2 paragraphs)
   - Prompt (exact)
   - AI result (structured list)

5. How to capture better notes

   - Use AI to clean up immediately after meeting

6. Templates for different meeting types
   - 1:1 sync
   - Team standup
   - Project planning

### **Capítulo 7: Cuidado - Cuándo NO Usar IA y Cómo Cuidar Tus Datos** (6.2K palabras)

**Objetivo de aprendizaje:** Use AI safely and know when to skip it

**Estructura:**
1. Privacy 101
   - What "public" AI tools can see
   - What stays private (depends on the tool)
   - Examples of what NOT to paste

2. Data types to NEVER paste into free AI
   - Passwords, API keys, credentials
   - Social security numbers, passport numbers
   - Medical information
   - Financial account numbers
   - Client contracts with confidential terms
   - Company trade secrets
   - Personal information about employees

3. When AI gets it wrong
   - Hallucination (making up facts)
   - Outdated information (trained on old data)
   - Misunderstanding context
   - Toxic output (bias in training data)

4. Tasks that NEED human judgment
   - Legal documents (consult lawyer first)
   - Medical advice (consult doctor first)
   - Financial planning (consult advisor first)
   - Firing someone (consult HR first)
   - Handling customer complaints (add personal touch)

5. Red flags: When to NOT trust AI output
   - It's inconsistent (different answer each run)
   - It references something "recent" but you suspect it's outdated
   - It's about very specialized knowledge (AI often guesses)
   - It sounds too good to be true (it might be)

6. The double-check process
   - If it's important: Verify facts independently
   - If it's legal: Have lawyer review
   - If it's financial: Have accountant review
   - If it's public: Spell-check + fact-check before publishing
   - If it's customer-facing: Add your personal touch before sending

7. Tools and their privacy policies
   - ChatGPT: OpenAI reads input to improve model (use paid version for privacy)
   - Google Gemini: Google reads input (use workplace version for privacy)
   - Microsoft Copilot: Microsoft's policies
   - Open-source tools: Run locally = maximum privacy

8. Company policies
   - Check your company's AI policy
   - Many companies ban using public tools with company data
   - Use enterprise versions if available
   - When in doubt, ask IT or legal

9. The law is evolving
   - EU has GDPR (strict data protection)
   - US has no unified law yet (varies by state)
   - Mexico: LFPDPPP (Mexico's GDPR equivalent)
   - Assume: If you wouldn't share it by email, don't paste into AI

10. Safe practices
    - Use a dedicated email for AI tools
    - Don't reuse passwords
    - Review AI's output before sharing
    - Assume nothing is truly private (even "private" tools)

**Ejercicio:** "Review your last 5 AI interactions. Did you paste anything you shouldn't have? If yes, what will you change?"

**Checklist:**
- [ ] I know what data types are risky
- [ ] I check company policy before using AI
- [ ] I fact-check important outputs
- [ ] I don't paste passwords, financial data, or confidential info
- [ ] I understand what the AI tool can see
